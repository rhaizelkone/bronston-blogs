The premise (spoiler-free)

The end of the world is just the beginning. Jaime, a web developer, is disillusioned by humanity's immersion in the digital world and his own hand in its creation. When a colleague returns from a survival school hidden in the Catskill Mountains, Jaime decides to go too.

There, Jaime learns to make fire with sticks, butcher animals, and craft a bow. All the while, the school's enigmatic founder, A, whispers of Day Zero, her prophecy that the world will end and be born anew. Then the day arrives, and civilization outside the school collapses, just as A had foreseen.

What worked

I finished this one in a day. That tells you most of what you need to know about the pace. It was fast paced and definitely kept me on my toes, and the whole time I was questioning what I would do in the main character's shoes. Not only would I be able to thrive on such a retreat, but would I survive it when worse came to worst?

The storyline was definitely attractive to me, disconnecting from the shackles we have become so accustomed to and actually cannot live without. The idea of stepping away from it all pulled me right in, and then having to survive when that escape turns dangerous kept me there. It is one thing to dream about unplugging. It is another to live it, with no backup plan and no way back to the life you left behind.

The ambiguity of the main character and his gender throughout the story was an interesting element, and I wondered if it was Jesse Sherwood's intentional design. It seemed to me like a mechanism to allow any reader to embody the main character or at least see themselves in his shoes more completely. I definitely fell right into character, and that made every choice feel closer, and every risk feel more personal.

It was a pretty straightforward read, and I mean that as a strength here. There was no getting lost, no stopping to work out what was happening. I could just settle in, keep turning the pages, and enjoy the ride for what it was.

What didn't land

My only critique would have been for a little more twist to the plot. I like my plot shaken not stirred. This one played it pretty straight, so if you are waiting for the story to swerve hard, you may find yourself waiting longer than you hoped.

That said, it did not take away from the ride. It just left me wishing Sherwood had shaken things up a little more along the way.

the verdict ✧

Should you read it?

Yes. If you love dystopian, wilderness survival thrillers that are fast paced and make you question what you would do to survive when everything falls apart, you will get a lot out of this. If you want a twisty, layered plot that keeps swerving, this one may feel too straightforward for you.

If you love dystopian stories, if you love wilderness survival, if you like a thriller you can finish in a day, if you enjoy questioning what you would do in the main character's shoes, if you want to disconnect from the shackles we have become so accustomed to, if you like a main character you can slip right into, or if you just want a fast, straightforward ride, then this, my midnight sleuth, is for you. 🌲

FINAL RATING: 3.5 OUT OF 5 SPINES
