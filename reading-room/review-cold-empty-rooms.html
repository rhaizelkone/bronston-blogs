The premise (spoiler-free)

Deep in the woods sits an old manor that only those who truly need it can find. Soren inherits it from his aunt, and he and Mara move in, finally having a home of their own. But the house has a presence of its own, an entity called the Great Other, and it starts sending messages. It tells Soren to invite others in and relieve them of their sorrow, and Mara is the one who has to descend into the home's darkest halls to earn its solace.

At its heart, this is a story about grief and sorrow and the search for a home, and about what happens when the place that promises you shelter starts making demands of its own.

What worked

This is like no other horror book I've read recently. Marino puts an interesting spin on occult horror, and it was trippy in a way that kept pulling me deeper even when I was not quite sure where it was going.

The dread is slow and creeping, and it builds the deeper Mara goes. Once I found my footing, it forced me to immerse myself in those cold empty rooms right along with Mara and Soren.

The house also brings together an eclectic mix of characters, and I didn't even bother trying to get into these characters' heads. They are as unhinged and twisted as the Great Other permits. I enjoyed the unraveling of Mara's backstory, in particular. As the story goes on, more of who she is and what she has carried with her comes to light.

What didn't land

Initially, it is difficult to orient yourself with the story. For a while, you are wondering what the heck is happening and why it even matters.

At times, the writing felt unnecessarily wordy. It did not take away from the atmosphere, but it did force me to go back and reread, which made some stretches feel longer than they needed to be.

the verdict ✧

Should you read it?

Yes, with a caveat. If you love haunted houses, creeping dread, and horror that gets under your skin, and you don't mind being confused for a while before it all starts to come together, you'll get a lot out of this. If you want fast, clear horror from page one, this one may test your patience.

If you've ever been haunted by grief, if you've searched for a home that felt safe, if you love haunted houses, if you like your dread slow and creeping instead of loud, if you enjoy occult horror with a twist, if you don't mind an eclectic, unhinged cast, or if you're happy to reread a passage to find your footing, then this, my midnight sleuth, is for you. 🏚️

FINAL RATING: 3.5 OUT OF 5 SPINES
