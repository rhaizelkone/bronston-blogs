The OSSD, explained

for families · november 2026 · 4 min read

Thirty credits, forty hours, one literacy requirement. Once you can see the pieces, the Ontario Secondary School Diploma stops feeling like a maze and starts looking like a map.

First, what is the OSSD?

The Ontario Secondary School Diploma, usually shortened to OSSD, is Ontario's official high school diploma. It is the credential students earn when they complete Ontario's secondary school requirements, and it is the diploma colleges, universities, apprenticeship programs and employers expect to see.

There is no separate online version and no different diploma for a different kind of school day. Whether courses are taken in a classroom, online, or a mix of both, students are working toward the same diploma and the same requirements.

The one thing to know first: what a student needs depends on the year they started Grade 9. Ontario updated the requirements for students who began Grade 9 in 2024, so two siblings can be following slightly different checklists. Both are correct.

Thirty credits, split two ways

Every student earns 30 credits in total. One credit is usually one full course. What changed in 2024 is how many of those credits must be in set subjects, and how many are free choice.

Started Grade 9 in 2023 or earlier

  • 30 credits in total
  • 18 compulsory credits, 12 optional
  • Literacy requirement
  • At least 2 online learning credits
  • 40 hours of community involvement

Started Grade 9 in 2024 or later

  • 30 credits in total
  • 17 compulsory credits, 13 optional
  • Literacy requirement
  • Financial literacy requirement, for students who began Grade 9 in 2025-26 or later
  • At least 2 online learning credits
  • 40 hours of community involvement

The compulsory credits

For students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, the 17 compulsory credits break down like this.

The core subjects

  • 4 in English, one per grade
  • 3 in mathematics: Grade 9, Grade 10, and one in Grade 11 or 12
  • 2 in science
  • 1 in Canadian history, in Grade 10
  • 1 in Canadian geography, in Grade 9
  • 1 in French as a second language
  • 1 in the arts
  • 1 in health and physical education
  • 0.5 in career studies
  • 0.5 in civics and citizenship

The newer pieces

  • 1 in technological education, in Grade 9 or 10
  • 1 from the STEM-related group: business studies, computer studies, cooperative education, or an extra mathematics, science or technological education credit

Students who started in 2023 or earlier follow the earlier list instead: the same core subjects, with one credit from each of three subject groups in place of the technological education and STEM-related credits above.

Optional credits are where interests live

The remaining 12 or 13 credits are chosen by the student. This is where a timetable starts to reflect a person: extra sciences for a health pathway, business courses, arts, languages, technology, or courses that simply keep curiosity alive. Optional does not mean unimportant. For many post-secondary programs, the optional credits are the ones that matter most.

Three requirements that are not courses

1. The literacy requirement

Most students meet this by passing the Ontario Secondary School Literacy Test (OSSLT), usually written in Grade 10. Passing the test is not the only route. Students who do not pass can meet the requirement another way, including the Ontario Secondary School Literacy Course. A missed test is a detour, not a dead end.

2. Forty hours in the community

Every student completes 40 hours of community involvement before graduating, and students can start collecting hours in the summer before Grade 9. The hours are meant to be spread through the high school years, not rescued in the final month, so starting early and keeping a simple record makes this the easiest requirement of all.

3. The financial literacy requirement

This one is new, and the dates matter. It applies to students who began Grade 9 in 2025-26 or later, and it takes effect in the 2026-27 school year. Students meet it inside their Grade 10 Career Studies course by earning at least 70% on the financial literacy modules, with two attempts to complete it. Families of younger students should expect it; families of older students can check it off the worry list.

Learning online still leads to the same diploma

At least two of the 30 credits must be online learning credits for students who started Grade 9 in 2020-21 or later, with an exemption process available through the school. Beyond those two, online courses count toward the diploma in the same way as any other credit. What matters is the credit, the curriculum behind it, and steady progress, not the room the learning happened in.

A simple family checklist

  • Confirm which checklist applies: the Grade 9 start year decides it.
  • Map the compulsory credits across all four years, not one year at a time.
  • Keep a running record of community involvement hours from Grade 9 on.
  • Ask guidance early about the literacy test, online credits, and any course a future program expects to see.

Sources: Government of Ontario, “Earning your high school diploma,” ontario.ca, updated November 18, 2026; Ontario Schools, Kindergarten to Grade 12: Policy and Program Requirements, section 6.1. Requirements can change, so confirm your student’s checklist with guidance.
