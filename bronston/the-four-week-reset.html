The Four-Week Reset

getting ready for september · august 2026 · 4 min read

September isn't tomorrow. But it's close enough now that you can feel it in the back of your head — that small, ignorable hum that says you should probably do something about this. Here's our advice: do something small, four times, and arrive in September like a person who has slept. Because Bronston isn't one building on one schedule. Some of you are twenty minutes from campus. Some of you are logging in from a time zone where first period lands squarely in the middle of your evening. The reset looks different depending on where you're sitting — but the four things are the same.

Weeks four and three: fix the clock

This is the one everybody skips and everybody regrets. Nobody goes from a 2 a.m. summer bedtime to a functioning first period in one night. Bodies don't do that. What they do do is move about fifteen to twenty minutes at a time.

So: shift your bedtime and your wake-up fifteen minutes earlier every couple of days, starting now. Four weeks of that is a two-hour swing, painlessly. Light helps — get outside in the morning, dim things down in the evening, and put the phone somewhere that isn't your pillow.

If you're joining us from overseas, the math is different but the principle holds: figure out which of your hours have to be awake-and-thinking hours, and start walking your sleep toward them now — not in the first week of class, when you also have homework.

Week two: the tech check

Not glamorous. Absolutely the thing that ruins a first day.

Log in to everything now, while nobody is waiting on you — your student account, your email, and your course site at virtualcourse.bronston.ca. Password forgotten? Great. Find that out on a Tuesday in August, not at 8:58 a.m. in September.

Then check the boring hardware: does your mic work, does your camera work, do your headphones work with the actual device you'll be using. Run a speed test at the hour you'll usually be in class, not at 3 a.m. when the whole house is asleep and everything looks perfect. And if your connection has opinions, have a plan B ready — a phone hotspot, a second location, someone's kitchen table.

Last thing: pick your spot. The place you'll actually be sitting for class. Clear it off. You'll be there a lot.

Week one: the soft launch

The week before, don't do everything — do three small things.

Get your supplies sorted, whatever that means for you: a notebook you like enough to open, pens that work, a bag that isn't still full of last June. Put your timetable somewhere your eyes already go — wall, phone lock screen, front of the notebook — and read it until you know it. And open one thing that makes your brain work: a book, a problem set, a language app, anything that isn't scrolling. Fifteen minutes. You're just reminding your head how thinking feels.

Then stop.

Rest is part of the plan, not a failure of it.
