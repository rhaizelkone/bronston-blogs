Press Play, Then Prove It

new in our courses · november 2026 · 4 min read

There is a particular kind of video every online student knows well. You press play, it plays, you nod along, it ends, and then the assignment arrives and asks a quiet and terrible question. Did any of that actually stick? We have been thinking about that a lot at Bronston, and our courses are changing because of it.

Videos that talk back, politely

Unit by unit, the old YouTube videos in our courses are being removed and replaced with Bronston interactive course videos. These are our own videos, made to point you straight to the work your teachers have prepared in the PDF lessons, rather than sending you off to learn a topic in someone else's order, with someone else's examples.

They are posted ahead of the related coursework, so the video is not an extra thing to hunt down after the fact. It is the front door to the lesson.

And they do not just play at you. As you watch, pop quiz questions pause the story and ask you to answer before you move forward. That small interruption is the whole point. You find out in the moment whether the idea landed, while the explanation is still fresh and you can go back thirty seconds instead of three days.

Meet the formative check

The second change has a new name. What many students knew as formative assessments are now called formative checks, and they matter in a new way.

A formative check is a short checkpoint after the learning, designed to answer one fair question. Have you gained a good enough understanding of this work to build the next part on it?

Students now need to complete the formative check and score at least 60 percent before progressing. That is different from before, when students could move forward whether the formative work went well or not. Moving on while confused rarely felt like freedom for long. It usually just meant the next lesson had to carry the weight of the last one too.

Here is the reassuring part. A formative check is not a trap door. Students can complete multiple formative checks before progressing, and unit assignments have been simplified. The goal is not to catch you out. The goal is to catch a misunderstanding early, while it is still small and fixable, and give you another run at it.

In later units, students who finish the formative questions may also see a note inviting them to try more questions if they wish. Those extra questions are optional practice, there for students who want more confidence or more time with a topic.

Learning you can check is learning you can trust.
— the heart of the change

What has not changed

Good changes should be clear about what they leave alone, so here is that part plainly.

These updates do not change work students have already done, and they do not change units already completed. They do not change course lesson plans or the strands students need to complete. They arrive unit by unit, ahead of new coursework, so what changes is the support around the learning that is still to come.

Think of it as a better path to the same destination. The video explains in Bronston's own voice. The questions inside it check that the explanation worked. The formative check confirms you are ready for what comes next. And your teacher's lessons remain at the centre of it all.
