A Parent's Guide to Online Learning

for families · november 2026 · 4 min read

When your child learns online, the school day happens in your home, and that changes your job in ways nobody hands you a manual for. You are not the teacher, and you are not supposed to be. But you are also not a bystander. You are the person who sets the stage, keeps the rhythm, and notices first when something is drifting. This small guide is about that middle job, the one parents of online learners actually hold, and how to do it well without turning your kitchen table into a second classroom.

Set the stage, then step back

The most helpful things a parent can do happen before the laptop even opens. A regular start time, even a loose one, tells the brain that learning has a beginning. A usual spot for schoolwork, with the charger, the notebook and the water bottle nearby, saves the daily treasure hunt that eats the first twenty minutes. Sleep does more for a morning lesson than any app, and a phone resting in another room during study time is a gift to every teenager, even the ones who argue otherwise.

Then, the harder part. Once the stage is set, step back. Online courses are built for the student to drive. At Bronston, the lessons themselves do a good deal of the guiding. Our interactive videos pause and ask questions the student must answer before moving on, and our formative checks ask for at least 60 percent before a student progresses, with more than one attempt allowed. The course is designed to catch drifting early. Your stepping back is not absence. It is trust, with a structure underneath it.

Check in like a coach

Coaches do not play the game for their athletes, and they do not only appear at the trophy ceremony either. They ask good questions at the right moments. What did you work on today is a better opener than did you finish, because it invites a story instead of a yes. Show me the part you are proud of works at every age. So does what felt hard this week, asked kindly and without a rescue plan already loaded.

Build one small ritual around progress. Once a week, sit together for ten minutes while your child walks you through their courses in Canvas, where they are in each unit, what their recent check scores look like, and what is coming next. Ten honest minutes a week will tell you more than a worried glance every day. And when your child is stuck on the learning itself, remind them of the help already inside their course. The Bronston AI Tutor sits at the bottom of every lesson page, teaches from the course in lesson order, can explain a topic in a fresh way, and can quiz them with five questions on the lesson in front of them. A stuck evening often ends there.

Know where the help lives

Every family hits a week where something is not working. A course feels too fast. A plan for university raises questions nobody at home can answer. The secret is not solving it all yourself. The secret is knowing which door to knock on, and knocking early.

Course questions belong in the course, where the teacher can see the exact lesson, or in a private message through the Canvas Inbox. Questions that are not about coursework, the ones about choosing courses, pathways, applications, scholarships and what comes after Bronston, belong in the Guidance Counsellor Hub in Canvas, where the Guidance Team answers Monday to Friday. And if your child is brand new to learning online, our free Foundations for Academic Success course was built to teach the habits themselves, from organising time to studying with purpose. It is a kind first step for the whole family.

One last thing, from us to you. Tell us early. A small message in week two is a conversation. The same message in the final week is an emergency, and emergencies are harder for everyone, most of all for your child. We would always rather hear the small version.
